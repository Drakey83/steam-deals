Why is an app needed for the cart?

Every browser enforces one rule: a website can never act as you on another website. If a page could add to your Steam cart, any page could also add to your Amazon cart, post as you on Facebook, or change your Steam account. Browsers can't tell a friendly cart-add from a hostile one, so they block all of it, and Steam offers no door around that. This is why every deal site stops at “View on Steam”.

The Steam Deals Windows app isn't a website. You sign into Steam inside it, on Steam's own login page, the same way the Steam client is signed in. That lets it talk to Steam's cart directly, so one click sends every game in your basket to your real Steam cart. You then pay in Steam as usual, on your PC or from the Steam app on your phone, because the cart belongs to your account.

Nothing is ever purchased by the app. It only adds items, and Undo takes them back out.

What you get

One-click Steam cart
Collect sales in the basket, see the total with an estimated tax, then send them all to your Steam cart at once.
Picks that fit you
It learns your taste from your library and playtime, hides everything you own, and tells you why each game matched.
Private by design
You sign in on Steam's page; the app never sees your password. Works even if your Steam profile is private. No telemetry.
Works with the website
Build a basket on the website or your phone, then send it to the app with one link or a six-letter code.

Install in a minute

  1. Download the installer with the button above.
  2. Run it. Windows SmartScreen will say the publisher is unknown, because the installer isn't code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Finish the installer. It adds a Start Menu entry and a desktop shortcut, and opens the app.
  4. Sign in through Steam in the app once. That's what makes the one-click cart possible.

The SmartScreen warning appears for any small app that hasn't paid for a code-signing certificate. The code is open source, so you can read exactly what it does.

Questions

Does it work on a phone?

Yes, with your PC doing the one click. Pair your phone once with the Windows app (the app shows a six-letter code; you type it into the website's basket on your phone). After that, “Send to my PC's Steam cart” on the phone puts the basket in your Steam cart within seconds, and because the cart is shared across devices you can pay right there in the Steam mobile app. Without pairing, each game in the basket has an “Open in Steam” button that opens its own Add to Cart button in the Steam app.

Does the app need to be running for the phone to work?

Yes. The PC does the adding, so Steam Deals has to be open there (it can sit in the background). If it isn't, the phone tells you, and the basket is added the moment you open the app.

Mac or Linux?

The one-click cart needs the Windows app for now. The website works everywhere, and you can run the app from source on any system with Node.js installed (see the GitHub page).

Can it buy anything without me?

No. It only adds games to your cart. Payment always happens in Steam's own checkout, with your confirmation.

Is my Steam account safe?

You sign in on Steam's own login page inside the app; the app never sees your password and only reads which games you own and how long you've played them. Your session, settings and caches stay in a folder on your PC, and signing out wipes the session.
